Corridors of Madness

Click a lit square to walk. Walk through a doorway into the dark and you draw the next room — doors only join where they line up. You have two actions a turn: a move of up to two squares, or investigating an encounter card on the square you are standing on. Entering a room turns its entrance card, which says where the encounter cards lie; those cards stay face down until an action is spent investigating one, unless an entrance card says otherwise. Turning a card over only shows you what it is — the investigating is what takes it up. A Clue gives its token and a Nothing gives nothing; a Monster, an Event or an Item brings a card out of its own deck — an Item is laid face up beside it and named, then goes into the hand when it is taken up, at no cost in actions. Seeing a card is not having it: one revealed across the room still has to be walked to and investigated. Entering a room turns an entrance card, even one you have already opened, but only once per investigator per turn. Every deck is shuffled back in when it runs out, apart from the rooms themselves — there are only so many of those. Health and Sanity come off the investigator sheets: Events and Items take and give both, and a ward takes the whole of the next loss. At 0 Health an investigator has fallen; at 0 Sanity they go mad and become a monster where they stood. Either way they are out, and the player picks a new investigator, who starts in the Foyer. Monsters come from the scenario's own deck: a Monster card turned over brings one onto its square, and the Wrench, Revolver, Knife and Holy Water can strike them. Attack a monster on or next to you (4-6 hits, 1-3 misses and hurts you); end your turn beside one, or be beside one when it appears, and you are engaged — then you may only Attack or Flee (4+ escapes, 1-3 hurts you). Monsters block movement. A few cards are still pending: they wait on a choice, another investigator, a die, a lock or the encounter deck put back in order, and they say so when they turn up instead of pretending to work.
